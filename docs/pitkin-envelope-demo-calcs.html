Why the calculation exists

One threshold decides whether your remodel is treated as a new house

The demo calculation sheets are not a demolition plan in the usual sense. They are an area accounting exercise whose only job is to prove which side of 75% your project falls on.

2021 IECC § R503.1.1, Exception 3

Alterations exposing or demolishing more than 75 percent of the building’s thermal envelope area shall comply with the energy requirements of Section R407 (new dwelling requirements).

Under 75%, ordinary alteration rules apply: envelope assemblies you touch comply with R402, and exposed envelope is brought to prescriptive code minimums unless the Chief Building Official grants a hardship. Over 75%, the entire dwelling has to meet new-construction energy requirements under R407, with REMP obligations following.

Local wording differs from the base code

Pitkin County rewrote R503.1.1 wholesale. The county’s adopted Exception 3 reads “alterations exposing more than 75% of the building’s thermal envelope area” — it drops “or demolishing.” The CBO’s policy memo quotes the base IECC text with both verbs. In practice the local test is exposure-centric, which matters for scopes that disturb assemblies without removing them.

The county amendment also requires pre- and post-construction Energy Rating Index assessments (ANSI/RESNET/ICC 301) on all alterations, to confirm the home does not perform worse than before. Like-for-like replacement work is excepted from the ERI requirement, provided building energy use is not increased.

Method

How the wall sheet is built

  1. Unfold the exterior walls into flat elevations and cut them into discrete panels, one per plane. The example uses 28 panels lettered A through AD — every jog and every gable face is its own panel.
  2. Tag every panel with its square footage on the drawing, and carry the same letters into a table with two columns: Individual Wall Area and Area of Wall to be Removed.
  3. Color-code each panel against the legend, and key the same conditions onto the floor plan with dashed linework.
  4. Count partial areas as partials. Panel H is 98.68 sf with only 22.58 sf counted; R is 245.70 sf with 7.50 sf; U is 221.06 sf with 15.00 sf. You tally the disturbed portion, not the whole panel.
  5. Sum the panels, then roll the wall totals into the combined figure with roof and floor, and print that combined total on all three sheets.
Counting rule

Walls with windows are counted gross

Openings are not deducted. Both columns — total area and removed area — are gross flat-plane elevation areas with glazing included. The county’s own drafting settles it three different ways.

QuantityBasis
Individual Wall Area
the denominator		Gross flat-plane elevation area. Window, door and garage door openings are not deducted. Gable ends measured as the true elevation shape, triangle included.

A window on its ownGets its own square footage only when the window is the element changing in a wall that otherwise stays. It already sits inside its panel’s gross area, so it is added to the removed column only — never to the total.

Why gross is the defensible basis

Per Chapter 2 of the 2021 IECC, the building thermal envelope is the set of assemblies that enclose conditioned space. A window is an envelope assembly with its own U-factor in Table R402.1.3 — not a hole in the envelope. Gross is also the only basis a reviewer can verify by scaling your elevation. Above all it must be consistent: the result is a ratio, so the same basis has to feed both columns.

Measurement conventions to hold
  • Measure to the outside of siding — the county’s example states this convention on the same sheet.
  • Flat plane, per plane. No unfolding shortcuts that merge two orientations into one panel.
  • Do not double count. The 3,324.01 sf wall total is the sum of gross panel areas and nothing else.
Edge cases

What counts as disturbance — including things you never touch

Exposing counts as demolishing

From the CBO’s policy: “Removal of interior wall finishes that expose wall and ceiling cavities even if existing framing remains shall be considered disturbance of the building thermal envelope and shall be included in the thermal envelope depiction and calculations.” Gut the drywall and that wall is in your numerator, framing intact or not. This is why the legend separates “wall to be removed” from “wall area to be removed.”

Unheated to heated conversion counts

Panels J, K, L, M and N carry an asterisk on the example sheet: “Area counted as demolition due to conversion from non-heated space to heated space & thermal envelope values change.” Bringing a garage, crawlspace or porch inside the envelope — or changing an assembly’s R-value — puts that area in the count even where no framing is disturbed.

Scope question

Like-for-like exterior siding does not count

Replacing exterior wood siding with the same product, without opening the cavity and without changing assembly values, stays out of the numerator. Three provisions agree.

  • The policy carves it out. Depiction plans are required for all envelope alterations “except for permits limited to exterior veneer, siding, window, or door replacement only,” and such replacement “does not, by itself, trigger new dwelling requirements or require exposure of interior wall cavities.”
  • The disturbance rule is about interior finishes. Pulling siding gets you to sheathing, not into the cavity.
  • The county’s amended R503.1.1 Exception 1 covers “the removal and replacement or the covering of existing materials, elements, equipment or fixtures using new materials, elements, equipment or fixtures that serve the same purpose.”

One ambiguity worth covering

The policy’s construction-document section asks for the area “proposed to be altered, exposed, or demolished.” “Altered” is broader than the code’s “exposing,” and an examiner could read a siding swap as an alteration. When siding work sits inside a larger project that already needs a depiction plan, draw those walls in the “existing wall to remain” fill, keep their square footage in the denominator, and carry a note:

Siding replacement is like-for-like. No cavity exposure and no change to assembly U-factor or R-value. Not counted as exposed thermal envelope area per R503.1.1 exception 1.

The memo is recent — dated 1/28/2026 — so this wording has little review history. Confirm the reading with the plans examiner before submitting.

Administration

When a depiction plan is required, and what can undo an approval

  • Required for all alterations affecting the existing thermal envelope, except permits limited to exterior veneer, siding, window or door replacement only.
  • Waivable for minor scopes. Where the CBO determines the work does not materially impact existing wall assemblies, detailed calculations may be waived under the exception to R103.1.
  • Show existing conditions and, where applicable, combined existing plus new construction — prepared so the department can clearly verify the total percentage being altered (R103.2.1 requires a depiction of the building thermal envelope).
  • Change orders can retroactively blow the threshold. A project approved under 75% that later exceeds it by change order must comply with the most recently adopted Pitkin County Energy Code, new-dwelling requirements included. At 68.70%, the example has about 580 sf of envelope in reserve.
  • Siding and window swaps have their own rule. R503.1.1.1: where all siding and windows are replaced on one or more sides, that envelope must comply with Table R402.1.3, and the applicant must demonstrate the existing cavity insulation R-value to size the continuous insulation.
Before you submit

Drawing checklist

  1. Three demo calc sheets — roof (flat plane), wall, floor — each carrying the combined running total, not just its own surface.
  2. Every envelope plane unfolded, lettered, and tagged with gross square footage; letters matching the table exactly.
  3. Legend with all five conditions: existing to remain, wall to be removed, wall area to be removed, thermal envelope change fill, thermal envelope change line.
  4. Asterisked notes explaining any area counted for conversion from non-heated to heated space or for assembly value change.
  5. Interior-finish removal areas shown as disturbed envelope, even where framing stays.
  6. Windows and doors drawn within panels; square footage on them only where the opening itself is changing.
  7. Like-for-like siding walls kept in the denominator with the exclusion note on the sheet.
  8. Linear-foot tallies for the Land Use Code determination kept visually separate from the energy calculation.
  9. Combined percentage stated plainly, with the roof, wall and floor subtotals shown so the arithmetic can be checked.
Verification

Every figure in this document was recomputed from the county’s example sheets and reconciles exactly: the 28 wall panels sum to 3,324.01 sf with 2,169.72 sf removed; the three surfaces sum to 9,208.41 sf with 6,326.54 sf removed, giving 68.70%; the starred conversion areas total 981.14 sf; and the linear tallies give 39.9% and 35.2%.
