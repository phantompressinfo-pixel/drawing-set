# Aspen & Pitkin Code Gem — how to use it

There is nothing to install. Open the link, type your question. That's it.

---

## WHAT IT'S FOR

- **Code questions** — "In Aspen, how is building height measured on a 6:12 roof?"
- **Checking your charts** — paste a screenshot of a calculation table and it
  re-adds every column and finds the errors
- **Building a submittal** — "What charts do I need for the demolition sheet?"
- **What goes where** — "What are the columns in the Height Over Topography table?"
- **Where to measure** — "Where exactly do I measure exposed subgrade wall?"

---

## THE ONE THING TO DO EVERY TIME

**Tell it the jurisdiction and the zone district in your first message.**

City of Aspen and unincorporated Pitkin County have completely different rules —
different height measurement, different floor area measurement, and a house in
the City uses the **IBC** while a house in the County uses the **IRC**. If you
don't say which, it will ask, but you'll save a round trip.

Good first message:

> *844 Example St, City of Aspen, R-15, single-family remodel with an addition.
> There is a P&Z Special Review resolution on the parcel. Here's my demolition
> chart — check my math.* [screenshot]

---

## SCREENSHOTS WORK — USE THEM

Paste a screenshot of a chart, a sheet, a plan, or a review comment. It will:
1. read back every tag and note it can see
2. verify the code citations against the actual code text
3. re-add every column and tell you which totals don't close
4. tell you what's missing from the sheet

It's at its best doing exactly this.

---

## GOOD QUESTIONS TO ASK IT

- "Does the attic count toward floor area? It's unfinished and the only access
  is a ceiling hatch with a pull-down ladder."
- "My basement is mostly buried. Check this exposed wall calculation."
- "What's the adjustment factor for an 8:12 roof and why?"
- "The City commented 'demonstrate how approvals are being met in the table.'
  What do they want?"
- "We're over the floor area cap. What are my legitimate options?"
- "What do I need on the site plan that reviewers always ask for?"

---

## THINGS IT WILL DO THAT MIGHT SURPRISE YOU

**It will ask if there's a prior land use approval.** Answer it. If there's a
P&Z or HPC resolution on the parcel, that resolution's floor area and height
numbers are the real limit — they beat the zone district numbers. Finding this
out late has cost this office weeks.

**It will refuse to give you IBC or IRC dimensions.** Egress window sizes, guard
heights, stair dimensions — it will name the section and send you to **UpCodes**,
which the office pays for. That's deliberate: those codes are copyrighted and
aren't in its files, so anything it "remembered" would be a guess. Guessed
dimensions on a permit set are exactly what we're avoiding.

**So the workflow is two tools, and they don't overlap:**
- **The Gem** — Aspen and Pitkin *zoning*: floor area, height, demolition,
  mitigation, setbacks, chart checking. UpCodes does not carry this.
- **UpCodes** — the *building code* text: IBC for city projects, IRC for county.
The Gem tells you which section to open; UpCodes shows you what it says.

**It will tell you when the code is ambiguous** and say to get it in writing from
the Zoning Officer (`comdevzoning@gmail.com`) instead of picking an answer for
you. Take that advice — it's usually about attic exemptions, roofs claimed as
"built over," and demolition exemptions.

---

## HOW MUCH TO TRUST IT

It searches the **actual text** of the Aspen and Pitkin codes and quotes it with
citations. That part is solid — and if it ever says something without a citation,
ask for one.

But it's a **first check, not the last word.** Before anything goes on a stamped
sheet:
- read the code section it quoted, yourself
- confirm the arithmetic
- confirm the code is still current (its files were captured **July 2026**)

If it can't find something, it will say so. Believe it — that means look it up
manually, not that the rule doesn't exist.

---

## IF IT SEEMS WRONG

Tell it. "Are you sure? Check the code text again." It responds well to being
pushed, and it will re-search rather than defend itself. If it's still wrong,
tell whoever owns the Gem — the method files may need a fix.
