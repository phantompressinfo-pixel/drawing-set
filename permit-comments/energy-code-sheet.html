Start here

The sheet SPM sent you is a good template and a bad fit in exactly two places. It is built on the 2024 IRC and a housewrap or drywall air barrier. Aspen is on the 2021 I-codes, and your barrier is spray foam. Everything else on it transfers directly — and the part worth copying is not the details, it is the structure: one sheet where a reviewer can find the climate zone, the compliance path, the R-values, the barrier method, the line-type key, the ventilation numbers and the envelope traced on a plan, without opening another sheet.

That sheet is what you are missing. You have the sections and you have the details. What you do not have is the page that ties them to the code, and that is the page a plans examiner reads first.

The sheet plan

Same organization as the template, with the blocks changed to match your project and your method. Red-outlined blocks are the ones that have no equivalent anywhere in your set today.

Three changes from the template you have to make, not optional:

Climate zone and code yearBoth are wrong on the template
  1. Aspen is Climate Zone 7, not 5B. Pitkin County reads as Zone 7 in IECC Table R301.1, which is why the reviewer's window comment asked for U-0.26 and not U-0.30. Zone 7 also drives ceiling R-60, floor R-38 and a −15°F winter design temperature. If you copy 5B off the template every R-value under it is wrong.
  2. The code year is 2021, not 2024. Aspen adopted the 2021 I-codes under Ordinance #1, Series 2023, with local amendments. The template says 2024 IRC. Change every reference, and add a line saying local amendments govern where more restrictive — that is what makes the 1.5 ACH50 number defensible on the sheet.
  3. The air barrier method block has no box for what you are doing. The template offers exterior sheathing, housewrap, sealed drywall, or aerobarrier. None of those is your answer. Replace the block with the by-zone designation you already committed to in note 20 — foam in framed assemblies, waterproofing below grade, MM6125 at the green roof, self-adhered membrane at the transitions. That block is the answer to the reviewer's “pick one and stick to it.”
Blocks the template does not haveAdd these
  1. Envelope and duct testing. The template has no blower door block at all, because most jurisdictions take the IECC default. Aspen does not — you are at 1.5 ACH50 and that number needs to be printed with its test standard and who performs it. Add duct testing per R403.3.5 and note it is waived where all ducts are inside the envelope, which yours are.
  2. Keynote legend and detail index. Your sections carry keynotes 1 through 10 in squares and detail bubbles in circles, and nowhere in the set does a legend say what any of them mean. This is the single biggest coordination gap in what you sent, and it is covered in the next section.
  3. Table R402.4.1.1. Put the whole 16-row schedule on the sheet with the detail or note that satisfies each row. It is the checklist the reviewer is working from. Filling it in yourself is how you find out which rows you have not covered — and there are seven of them.

Numbering — fix this before anything else

This is the first-pass finding that matters most, because it is the one that gets the whole submittal kicked back without anyone looking at your details. Right now the sections and the detail sheet do not agree with each other.

The bubbles point at a sheet that is not the detail sheetSheet number conflict
  1. The sections call out G2.09. The details are titled G2.08. Your longitudinal and garage sections carry bubbles reading 2/G2.09, 3/G2.09, 4/G2.09, 5/G2.09, 6/G2.09 and 7/G2.09, and the note on the section says SEE DETAILS ON SHEET (G2.09). Every detail on the detail sheet is tagged G2.08 in its title bubble. One of the two is wrong.
  2. And one bubble in the same section says G2.08. The crawl space bubble at the bottom of both sections reads 1/G2.08 while its neighbours read G2.09. So it is not even consistently wrong — it is mixed, which reads as a set that was not checked.
  3. The fix: sections stay on G2.08, details move to G2.09, and every detail title bubble gets G2.09 under the number. Then the note on the section is already correct.
Detail 6 is called out and does not existMissing drawing
  1. The garage section calls out 6/G2.09 at the garage roof. There is no detail 6 on the sheet. The details that exist are numbered 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8. Six is a hole.
  2. The detail that should be there is the roof-to-wall at an abutting roof — where the lower roof lands against a wall that keeps going up. That condition occurs at the garage and again at the family room. It matters because the wall cavities behind the abutting roof can neither be foamed nor inspected once that roof is framed, so the foam has to go in first. That sequencing note is the entire reason the detail exists.
  3. Alternatively, renumber the bubble to point at detail 8 if the garage roof really is the membrane assembly. But then detail 8 is called out by a bubble that says 6, which is the same problem in a different place. Draw the detail.
Keynotes and detail numbers are two systems that do not line upStructural confusion

Your sections use square tags 1 through 10 and circle tags 1 through 8, and they mean different things. Detail 8 on the detail sheet carries a square 9 inside the drawing. Detail 3 carries a square 4. A reviewer tracing keynote 9 has to guess that it lives in detail 8.

  1. Either collapse them into one system — keynote n is drawn at detail n, full stop — which is cleaner and is what the keynote legend block on the new sheet assumes.
  2. Or keep both and publish the crosswalk in the legend, so keynote 9 explicitly reads GARAGE / DWELLING SEPARATION . . . 7/G2.09.
  3. Either way the legend has to exist. Ten numbered flags with no legend anywhere in the set is a comment by itself.
Smaller cross-reference itemsCheck while you are in there
  1. GROUND AS INSULATION on the longitudinal section is carried over from the template, where it describes a slab on grade. Your lower level is a conditioned crawl with a slab, an under-slab vapor retarder and insulated perimeter walls. Relabel it, or a reviewer reads the two as contradicting each other.
  2. Detail 4 is drawn at 3" = 1'-0" and the rest at 1" = 1'-0". That is fine and normal, but check the printed scale actually matches the geometry — on the sheet you sent it reads closer to the others than a 3× blow-up should.
  3. Detail 8 is titled MEMBRANE ROOF but the roof assembly you handed me for it, R1/A5.02, is a standing seam metal roof over an insulated nail base panel. Those are two different drawings. Confirm which roof the keynote is pointing at — the redrawn version below is the membrane one, with the note set for it.
  4. The air barrier is red on the sections and blue foam on the details. Neither is explained anywhere. The thermal envelope key block on the new sheet fixes this, but the key has to appear on both sheets, not just the code sheet.

The seven details, redrawn

Each one below is your detail with the missing notes placed on it. The geometry is simplified — the point is what the notes say and what they point at, not the linework. Notes you already have are carried over verbatim so you can see what is new.

1 — crawl space slab at foundation wallFour callouts missing
  1. Nothing is called out for the insulation. The wall has a vinyl blanket and 2" of rigid on the exterior face per W1/A5.00, and neither appears on the detail. Add VINYL BLANKET INSULATION, R-19 MIN. TAPE ALL JOINTS — SEE W1/A5.00 and 2" RIGID INSULATION, R-10 MIN. CONTINUOUS TO FOOTING. Table R402.1.2 wants R-15 continuous or R-19 cavity at a crawl space wall in Zone 7, so the two together are what makes the row pass.
  2. The perimeter drain is drawn and unnamed. PERIMETER DRAIN, REF CIVIL.
  3. The slab has no callout. 4" CONCRETE CRAWL SPACE SLAB, REF STRUCT, and name the gravel below it as a capillary break.
  4. The vapor retarder leader points only at the turn-up. It reads as if the note describes the vertical leg. Point it at the horizontal run under the slab, or add a second arrowhead — the note covers both.
  5. The note that closes the argument is not there. The waterproofing is on the outside face and the vapor retarder is on the inside face. Those are two different planes with a concrete wall between them, and unless you say the concrete is the continuity, a reviewer reads a break. Note 1 on the redraw says it explicitly.
  6. Add the crawl space status. CRAWL SPACE IS CONDITIONED AND UNVENTED PER IRC R408.3. NO FOUNDATION VENTS OCCUR. Without it, the reviewer has to assume vented, and a vented crawl puts your insulation in the wrong plane.
2 — green roof edge at foundation wallThe membrane is never named
  1. MM6125 appears in a note about lapping and nowhere as a callout. The membrane on the slab has no label of its own. Add MONOLITHIC MEMBRANE 6125 (.215") — DESIGNATED AIR BARRIER AT GREEN ROOF, FULLY ADHERED TO SLAB. Right now the drawing shows a line and asks the reviewer to infer what it is.
  2. Nothing above the membrane is drawn. The Hydrotech build-up — vegetation, growing medium, GardenDrain GR25, root barrier, filter fabric — is what makes this a green roof rather than a terrace, and it is also what protects the barrier. Draw it and call it out.
  3. The foam has no R-value. CLOSED CELL SPRAY FOAM AT UNDERSIDE OF SLAB, R-50 MIN.
  4. Two notes are doing three jobs. The turn-down note currently also names the 8" slab. Split them — the slab gets its own callout with a structural reference.
  5. Add the sequencing note. INSTALL AND INSPECT BEFORE BACKFILL AND BEFORE PLACING DRAINAGE COURSE AND GROWING MEDIUM. Once the medium is on, nobody is inspecting that lap again.
  6. Add the corner note. MEMBRANE SHALL NOT BRIDGE A HARD 90°. CANT STRIP OR FILLET W/ REINFORCING FABRIC. You have it on detail 3 and not here, and it is the same membrane.
3 — return corner at green roofWell noted — four small things
  1. The MM6125 on the slab still has no callout here either. It is named inside the transition-membrane note. Give it its own leader, same as detail 2.
  2. The vegetation-free zone leader points at the concrete wall. It should point at the gravel border on the roof surface next to the wall — that is the thing being described. As drawn it reads as describing the foundation.
  3. Four leaders converge at the sill and cross each other, so it is not clear which note belongs to which joint. Fan them, or move two to the other side.
  4. No R-values anywhere on the detail. The wall foam and the slab foam both need one.
  5. Add the summary note tying the three materials into one plane: MM6125 at the slab, transition membrane to the wall WRB, wall WRB lapped to the spray foam at the gasketed sill. Three barrier callouts without that sentence read as three separate claims, and this detail is the one place in the set where the whole by-zone argument is visible in a single drawing. Make it say so.
4 — roof at ridgeMissing the code citation
  1. Nothing above the sheathing is drawn or named. The detail starts at the foam. Add ROOFING, UNDERLAYMENT AND SHEATHING — SEE ROOF ASSEMBLY, A5.02 so the detail connects to the assembly sheet.
  2. The ridge beam is not called out, which is odd given that a note describes foaming around it.
  3. IRC R806.5 is not cited. CONDITIONED ATTIC (UNVENTED) is a claim; R806.5 is the permission. Spell out the condition it turns on: air-impermeable insulation in direct contact with the underside of the structural roof sheathing. That sentence is the whole reason a closed-cell foam roof passes.
  4. Say there are no vents. NO RIDGE, SOFFIT OR GABLE VENT OCCURS IN THIS ASSEMBLY. A reviewer looking at an unvented attic will look for the vents you did not draw; tell them there are none.
  5. No R-value and no thickness. Zone 7 wants R-60 at the ceiling. Add the assembly R-value and the 1-1/2" minimum for the air barrier function — those are two different numbers doing two different jobs.
  6. Add the inspection note. Foam gets inspected before the ceiling closes, or it does not get inspected.
5 — typical eave and sill plateThe handoff has no callout
  1. The barrier handoff at the sill is not noted. This is the joint between concrete and framing — the one place the below-grade barrier becomes the above-grade barrier — and the detail currently shows the sill without saying what happens at it. Add SEAL FOAM AND SILL GASKET TO FOUNDATION WATERPROOFING — TRANSITION FROM THE BELOW-GRADE TO THE ABOVE-GRADE BARRIER. That transition is keynote 3.
  2. The sill gasket is missing. You have GASKET SILL PLATE TO FOUNDATION on detail 3 and not on this one, and this is the typical condition.
  3. The soffit note is not there. SOFFIT SHALL NOT BE VENTED — UNVENTED ROOF ASSEMBLY. An eave detail with no soffit note is the first place a reviewer looks for a vent.
  4. The WRB is not called out. The wall has a self-adhered barrier and this detail never names it, so the drawing does not show where the foam hands off to the membrane.
  5. No assembly references and no R-values. Add SEE W_/A5.00 on the wall and the roof assembly reference at the rafters.
  6. Which line is the barrier? On detail 1 the dashed line is clearly the barrier. Here the dashed vertical reads more like a grid line. Same convention on every detail, and put it in the key.
7 — garage / dwelling separation with the chaseMissing most of keynote 10
  1. Say which wall carries the barrier. Foam is drawn in both walls, so the drawing offers two candidate planes and the reviewer will ask which one is designated. The dwelling-side wall is the envelope; the garage-side wall is fire separation. Label them, or the continuity argument has a fork in it.
  2. The chase is not labelled and nothing crosses it. Add the void label and show at least one duct and one pipe running from the chase into the dwelling. That crossing is the entire content of keynote 10, and combining the two details means it has to come with it.
  3. Seal each service individually. SEAL EACH SERVICE TO THE BARRIER W/ MEMBRANE COLLAR AND FIRE-RATED SEALANT. SEAL EACH ONE INDIVIDUALLY — not one seal around a shared sleeve, which is how it gets built if the drawing does not say otherwise.
  4. The 5/8" Type X gypsum is still not on the detail. This has been flagged twice now. It is a code requirement on the exact plane the detail is about — IRC R302.6 — and the sealed base is also what makes the air barrier continuous at the floor. It does two jobs and it is drawn as neither.
  5. The duct needs its code line. SHEET METAL DUCT, 26 GA MIN. NO OPENINGS INTO GARAGE — IRC R302.5.2.
  6. Two notes under the drawing. NO DOOR OR OTHER OPENING OCCURS IN THIS SEPARATION. and PENETRATIONS SHALL BE FIRE-SEALED PER IRC R302.11. The first closes the question before it is asked.
  7. Housekeeping. R-value on the foam, a W14/A5.00 reference, and retire keynote 10 from the legend once the details are merged — otherwise the legend points at a detail that no longer exists on its own.
8 — low slope / membrane roofOne note is doing all the work
  1. Confirm which roof this is. The title says MEMBRANE ROOF; the assembly you gave me for it, R1/A5.02, is standing seam metal over an insulated nail base panel. If it is the metal assembly, the top four callouts change to standing seam, VersaShield underlayment, 15/32" plywood and nail base panel — the foam callout and all five notes stay exactly as drawn.
  2. Nothing above the sheathing is named. One callout on the foam is the whole detail right now. Every layer above it needs a leader, or this is a diagram rather than a detail.
  3. Tape the existing sheathing seams. Whatever goes on top installs from above, which means the existing deck is exposed and reachable right now. Taping it costs almost nothing and gives you a continuous second plane at the exact interface between the two insulation layers. It is the cheapest redundancy in the whole set and it disappears the day the roofers show up.
  4. R-values. One for the insulation above deck and one for the total assembly. And check them against R1/A5.02, where R-60 currently appears twice — once on the panel and once as the total — which cannot both be true.
  5. Sequencing. The foam goes in from below, so the ceiling has to be open when the foam crew arrives. That is a fact about the job, not the drawing, and it belongs on the drawing.
  6. R806.5 again. Same unvented citation as detail 4.
