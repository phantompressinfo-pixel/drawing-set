# Detail types - what each one must show

Every type follows the same pattern:
- **Draw**: the elements that must be on the drawing.
- **Continuity**: the lines to trace across the joint.
- **Notes**: the usual design-intent notes. Assembly layers are never on this list,
  because the tag covers them.
- **Verify**: the questions to settle before issue.

Numbers marked *(office practice)* are the office's standard, taken from its issued
details. They are not code limits. Where a code limit applies, get it from the code
library for the jurisdiction.

Mountain-site conditions run through all of these: deep snow, ice damming, snow
shedding off metal roofs, freeze-thaw, high UV and steep grades. Always ask where
the snow goes.

---

## 1. Exterior details

Windows (head, jamb, sill), doors and thresholds, penetrations (vents, hose bibbs,
lights), cladding terminations, soffits, trim, railings on exterior walls.

**Draw**
- The wall tag and the unit or item.
- The rough opening, and the sill pan with back dam and end dams.
- Head flashing with end dams.
- Jamb flashing, the sealant joint and backer rod, and the interior air seal.
- Rainscreen closures (insect screen at the top and bottom of the cavity).
- Blocking for the unit and the trim.
- Drip edges.

**Continuity**
- Water: WRB shingle-lapped to the head flashing and the sill pan.
- Air: interior air seal around the whole perimeter of the unit.
- Thermal: the unit's position within the insulation plane.

**Notes**
- The install sequence: sill pan, unit, jambs, head, WRB flap down.
- Which way each lap runs.
- The unit's plane relative to the CI.
- Shim and fastening zones that must stay clear of the pan.
- The sight line or reveal the design wants.
- Mock-up of the first opening.

**Verify**
- Unit manufacturer and the installation method.
- Rough opening size.
- Snow load on sills and heads at grade-level and deck-level openings.
- Egress sill heights, from the code library.

## 2. Interior details

Stairs, guards and handrails, casework, ceiling and soffit transitions, rated wall
and floor joints, shower and wet-area floors, fireplaces.

**Draw**
- Wall, floor and ceiling tags.
- Finish-to-finish dimensions (not framing dimensions).
- Blocking.
- Reveals and shadow lines.
- Rated joints (firestop system, head of wall).

**Continuity**
- Fire and smoke separation at rated assemblies.
- Sound seals at demising walls.
- The waterproofing plane in wet areas, turned up at the walls.

**Notes**
- Dimensions to finish surfaces.
- Blocking for everything that attaches (rails, casework, grab bars, TVs, heavy
  fixtures).
- Scribe and reveal intent.
- No exposed fasteners.
- Acclimation and protection of finished work.
- Shop drawings.
- Coordination with interiors, electrical and MEP (devices kept out of rails and
  treads).

**Verify**
- Code limits (rise and run, guard and rail heights, openings) from the code library.
- On City of Aspen work these are IBC sections. See the stair and handrail sets on
  `stair-details-drawings` for the format.

## 3. Roof details

Eave, rake, ridge and hip, valley, headwall, sidewall, parapet, curb and
penetration, skylight, snow retention, gutters, and vented (cold) roof edges.

**Draw**
- Roof tag and wall tag.
- Edge metal with its cleat and drip.
- Underlayment at the edge.
- Vent path, intake and exhaust, for cold roofs.
- Snow retention.
- Gutter, or the snow-shed zone.
- Slope arrow and the T.O. roof elevation.

**Continuity**
- Water: underlayment into the gutter or over the drip.
- Air: roof air barrier to wall air barrier, the line that fails most often.
- Thermal: roof insulation to wall insulation with no gap at the plate.
- Vapor: the vent space kept outside the air barrier.

**Notes**
- Base flashing turn-ups (8" min. *(office practice)*).
- Counterflashing laps (4" min. *(office practice)*).
- Hemmed edges and end dams.
- Panel end hems into cleats; clips allowed to float.
- No exposed fasteners in panel pans.
- High-temperature underlayment at metal roofs and at the eaves (ice barrier extent
  from the code library; the jurisdictions differ).
- Snow retention clamped to the seams, never through the pan.
- Kickout flashing at the low end of every sidewall.

**Verify**
- Headwall or sidewall?
- Slope against the panel profile (hydrokinetic vs hydrostatic).
- Snow drift and where shed snow lands (doors, decks, walks, neighbours).
- Roof class and uplift.
- Ice barrier extent for the jurisdiction.

## 4. Foundation details

Footings and foundation drains, slab edges, below-grade walls and waterproofing,
crawlspaces, radon and vapor control under slabs, frost-protected edges, and window
wells.

**Draw**
- Foundation wall tag and slab/floor tag. A below-grade wall gets its own tag.
- Footing and drain with filter fabric and gravel.
- The drain's discharge path, noted.
- Waterproofing or dampproofing extent.
- Drainage board.
- Insulation extent at the slab edge.
- The vapor retarder under the slab.
- Finish grade and T.O. slab elevations.
- Waterstop at construction joints below grade.

**Continuity**
- Water: waterproofing to the drain, and the lap to above-grade WRB or flashing at
  grade.
- Air and soil gas: the under-slab vapor retarder sealed to the wall.
- Thermal: the slab-edge thermal break, with the wall insulation carried down.

**Notes**
- Laps and terminations of the waterproofing, and its protection during backfill.
- Drain slope and where it discharges.
- Insulation carried down to (__) below the slab edge.
- Seal every penetration through the vapor retarder.
- Backfill material and compaction per the geotechnical report.
- Sequence of waterproofing, then drainage board, then backfill.

**Verify**
- The geotechnical report's recommendations.
- Frost depth and footing depth per structural.
- Radon requirement for the jurisdiction, from the code library.
- Window well depth, and the ladder where the office position requires one (see the
  code library's building-code-by-jurisdiction notes).

## 5. Exterior transition details

Where one assembly or material becomes another:
- wall to roof;
- wall to foundation at grade;
- cladding change (stone to siding);
- wall to deck (ledger);
- wall to a projecting roof or canopy;
- a green roof to its edge;
- the base of a stone veneer.

This is the type the tag rule matters most for. Two or three tagged assemblies
meet, and every note is about the joint.

**Draw**
- A tag on *each* assembly, on each side of the transition.
- Everything that makes the joint:
  - flashings and transition membranes;
  - blocking or sub-girts;
  - shelf angles;
  - screens;
  - sealant joints;
  - protection board.
- Clearances, such as cladding to grade and cladding to the roof surface.

**Continuity**
- All four control layers, traced one at a time from one assembly into the other.
- If you can't draw one, the detail isn't finished.

**Notes**
- Which layer laps over which, and by how much.
- What goes in first.
- Clearances with a reason ("NO LEDGE TO CATCH WATER", "KEEP STONE OFF THE ROOF").
- Every flashing, flagged.
- Mock-up.

**Verify**
- Which way the slope runs at the transition.
- Support for the heavier cladding at the change.
- Grade elevations along the whole wall.
- Deck ledger flashing and attachment per structural.

Examples:
- `examples/wall-at-foundation.py`, wall to foundation at grade.
- 6/A6.01 and 7/A6.01 on branch `admiring-galileo` (stone wall to standing seam
  roof, and green roof edge).

## 6. Wall sections

A full-height cut, footing to roof, usually at 3/4" = 1'-0" or 1/2" = 1'-0". **A wall
section is a map, not an enlarged detail.**

**Draw**
- Every assembly tag, at each level where it occurs.
- Level lines with elevations: T.O. footing, T.O. slab, finish floors, plate, and
  T.O. roof / ridge.
- Floor-to-floor dimensions.
- A callout bubble (`d.callout(...)`) at every joint that has an enlarged detail:
  - eave;
  - window head and sill;
  - floor line;
  - grade;
  - footing.
- Code-driven heights at the section scale: guard heights, sill heights, and the
  roof height against the zoning limit. Take the values from the code library.

**Continuity**
- The control layers shown continuous at section scale.
- The enlarged details prove each joint.

**Notes**
- Very few. The callouts point to where the notes live.
- Note only what exists at section scale:
  - "ALIGN WINDOW HEADS";
  - "HOLD FLOOR-TO-FLOOR";
  - "HEIGHT MEASURED PER ZONING - SEE A0.__".
- Never the layers. The tags cover them.

**Verify**
- Heights against zoning, from the code library (height is measured differently in
  the City and the County).
- Every callout has a matching detail.
- Tags agree with the schedule.
